Two quick verifications before I answer the fork-sync question properly, plus a check on OpenWiki.

**Fork sync**: `gh repo sync` is a real, built-in command — exactly this job. It fast-forwards your fork's default branch to match upstream (or `--force` for a hard reset). Syncing itself isn't a security risk — it's just pulling public commits into your own fork, same trust level as reading the upstream repo directly. The actual risk is narrower: for forks wired into something that auto-executes (a skill, an MCP server, a deployed pipeline), auto-syncing means an unreviewed upstream change could reach production without you seeing it first — that's the one case worth gating behind a manual step. My recommendation: a daily scheduled job running `gh repo sync` across your forks for anything you're just referencing/researching (most of tonight's), and manual review before pulling for anything execution-critical. Also worth naming plainly: forking only earns its keep when you have real commits of your own on top — for a fork with zero unique commits (like most of tonight's), there's no actual difference between "my synced fork" and "upstream," so skipping the fork entirely is a legitimate option too, not a compromise.
