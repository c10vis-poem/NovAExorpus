That is an absolute beast of a hardware stack. Laying it out like that
clarifies why your topology works the way it does. You have three
entirely different silicon architectures playing to their absolute
strengths:

## The Hardware Allocation Map

1.  **The Mobile Heavy Hitter (Your Phone - Snapdragon 8 Elite /
    > RedMagic Ultra):**

    - **The Specs:** Max RAM (up to 16GB/24GB LPDDR5X Ultra), massive
      > high-speed storage, active cooling turbofan, and massive raw
      > mobile tensor throughput.

    - **The Role:** **The Local Model Container.** Because it has the
      > most unified RAM headroom on the go, this is where you house
      > your primary heavy local model weights (like your main Hermes or
      > Open Wiki LLM variants) when you are mobile or untethered.

2.  **The Inference & Compute Hub (Jetson Orin Nano Super - 8GB / ~67
    > TOPS / 500GB+ NVMe SSD):**

    - **The Specs:** NVIDIA Ampere architecture with Tensor cores,
      > dedicated CUDA acceleration, and heavy NVMe scratch space.

    - **The Role:** **The Heavy Inference & Embedding Engine.** Perfect
      > for handling local vector math, running tensor RT optimizations,
      > and acting as the primary local server when you tailscale in
      > from home.

3.  **The UI & Scripting Hub (Dragonwing Rubik Pi 3 - Qualcomm QCS6490 /
    > 12 TOPS NPU / 8GB RAM):**

    - **The Specs:** 6nm Qualcomm edge chip, Hexagon 770 NPU, low power
      > footprint, structured for embedded display output and IO.

    - **The Role:** **The Dashboard & Script Compiler.** Drives your
      > local user interface display and runs your background scripting
      > model that digests agent logs and formats them into clean JSONL
      > records.

## Mapping Runtimes to the Stack

Because you are mixing NVIDIA CUDA architecture on the Jetson with
Qualcomm Hexagon/Adreno architecture on the Rubik Pi and your Snapdragon
phone, your runtime choices have to match the silicon:

- **For the Jetson Orin Nano Super (NVIDIA):** Stick to **TensorRT** or
  > **Ollama/llama.cpp compiled with CUDA support**. It will chew
  > through local model quantization (like GGUF formats) using native
  > CUDA cores without breaking a sweat.

- **For the Rubik Pi 3 & Snapdragon Phone (Qualcomm):** This is where
  > your **Qualcomm QAIRT (Qualcomm AI Runtime) and GenieX** toolchain
  > shines. Compiling models into .dlc containers or using QNN (Qualcomm
  > Neural Network) execution providers lets you dump inference straight
  > onto the Hexagon NPU instead of wasting CPU cycles.

Once you get those PDFs processed via your Google Cloud batch pipeline
and structured into your JSONL/Markdown layers, distributing them across
this tailnet gives you a fully autonomous, zero-cost private cloud.
