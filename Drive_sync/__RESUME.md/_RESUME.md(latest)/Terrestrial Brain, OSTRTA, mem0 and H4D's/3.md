Yes, the task-observer includes an interactive human-in-the-loop gating framework
designed exactly for this. By default, it never applies updates automatically. It stages them
silently in your workspace directory, forcing Claude Code to display a complete breakdown for
your manual review and approval before anything propagates into your system. [1, 2, 3, 4]
The workflow maps out directly into your TUI terminal through two core mechanics:
1. The Dynamic Task Gating Interface
Whenever you boot a new task session—and at least 7 days have passed with open logs—the
task-observer intercepts Claude's initialization. It prints a clean terminal interactive grid: [2, 5]
●​
●​ It surfaces every accumulated observation grouped cleanly by skill.
●​ It forces Claude to pause execution entirely and wait for your explicit confirmation.
●​ You can accept, reject, or manually adjust individual behaviors before the underlying code
changes ever touch your repo. [2]
●​
2. The Local Staging Architecture
If you don't want to wait for the automatic weekly review cycle to trigger, you can browse and
manage the raw staged changes natively at any time inside your project tree: [1, 4, 5]

# View pending updates the observer has compiled from your corrections​
cat skill-updates/proposed-changes.md​
​
# View the raw chronological history tracking your hot/cold memory states​
cat skill-observations/observation-log.md​

Nothing modifies your active folders or your personal forks behind your back. The task-observer
logs the raw behavioral signals completely passively, then holds the resulting skill modifications
in the skill-updates/ staging zone as a literal pull-request review layout waiting on your terminal
command. [1, 3, 4]
