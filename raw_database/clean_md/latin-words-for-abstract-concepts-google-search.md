Latin words for future or opportunities or pathways or birth

The key Latin terms for future, opportunities, pathways, and birth include futurum (the future), occasio (opportunity/chance), via or iter (path/way), and ortus or natus (birth/origin).

Future
  Futurum: The future, or that which is to come.
  Futura: The plural form, often used to describe a broad field of unrealized future events.
  Posteritas: Posterity or future generations.

Opportunities
  Occasio: A favorable moment, occasion, or opportunity.
  Potestas: Power, ability, or opportunity/permission to do something.
  Copia: Abundance, supply, or a chance/means to access something.

Pathways
  Via: A road, path, street, or a figurative way of life.
  Iter: A journey, march, or specific route/pathway.
  Limes: A boundary line, track, or crossroad path.

Birth
  Ortus: Birth, origin, or the rising of a celestial body.
  Natus: Born (past participle of nascor, to be born).
  Nativitas: Birth or native condition (later/medieval Latin).

The letter G is 100% Roman/Latin, but its original substitute was the letter C.

The History: Why "C" is the Original "G"
The early Romans inherited their alphabet from the Etruscans. The Etruscans didn't have a "hard G" sound, so they used the letter C to represent both the hard "K" sound and the "G" sound.

For centuries, early Latin didn't have the letter G at all. Famous Roman names like Gaius and Gnaeus were explicitly written as CAIVS and CNAEVS. It wasn't until around 230 BCE that a Roman schoolteacher named Spurius Carvilius Ruga added a tiny stroke to the bottom of the C, inventing the "G" used today.
